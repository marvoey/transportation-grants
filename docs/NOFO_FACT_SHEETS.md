# Fact sheets for the five prepared funding opportunities and their downloaded notices

Written 2026-11-11 from the notice documents in `data/opportunities/`, to ground fictitious test projects. Each fact carries a **PDF page number** (`p.N`), the page numbering the app's **View source** uses; the number printed on a notice page can differ from it by one. **A person has not verified any of this.** It is my reading of the notices, and the app's extracted requirements (also unreviewed) were used only as pointers to where to look.

**A correction to numbers seen earlier today.** The Simpler.Grants.gov listing shows an award "floor" and "ceiling" for some programs, but those can be the notice's *total pool* rather than a per-award range. The notices themselves say: **Recalls** anticipates 3 to 5 awards of **$300,000 to $500,000 each** (from $1.5 million in total), not "$300,000 to $1.5 million"; **ITSE** anticipates 8 to 15 awards of **$500,000 to $2,000,000 each** (from $20 million in total), not "$300,000 to $20 million". The sheets below use the notice figures.

**How to read the "gaps".** A gap is something a real applicant could still be missing while the project otherwise fits. Every gap is a *preparation* gap (fixable before applying), not an eligibility conflict, because the samples are meant to be projects that **would apply**.

## 1. FY2026 Wildlife Crossings Pilot Program (FHWA-WCPP-26-002)

**Federal Highway Administration · posted · closes November 16, 2026, 11:59 PM EST (p.3, p.11) · about $80 million in FY2026, no minimum or maximum award (p.3, p.8) · awards announced March 2027 (p.3)**

**Who can apply (p.3-4):** a State DOT, a metropolitan planning organization, a unit of local government, a regional transportation authority, a special purpose district or public authority with a transportation function, an Indian Tribe, a Federal Land Management Agency, or a group of these. Joint applications need a lead applicant and a description of each partner's role (p.5). Eligible *partners* also include foundations, nongovernmental organizations and universities (p.5).

**Rules that can make an application ineligible:**
- **Cost share:** a non-Federal share of at least **20 percent** from every recipient, or the application is ineligible (p.4, p.6). It may be State, local or private money. Costs already incurred or funds already spent do not count (p.6). Certain safety construction projects can have a 100 percent Federal share, and States with Federal and Indian lands get a sliding-scale adjustment (p.6). Tribes and Federal Land Management Agencies may use other Federal funds as match (p.4).
- **State highway agency consultation:** an applicant other than a State DOT or Federal Land Management Agency must include **documentation that the State highway agency was consulted** while developing the application (for Tribes, only if the project is not on Tribal lands). Applications without it are not reviewed (p.5).
- **State DOT administration:** for any recipient other than a Tribe or Federal Land Management Agency, the **State DOT must administer the grant**, and FHWA cannot award if the State DOT does not agree (p.6).

**What it funds (p.3, p.7, p.12-14):** projects that reduce wildlife-vehicle collisions and improve habitat connectivity. **Construction and non-construction** projects are both eligible, and construction may be prioritized (p.4). Research projects are contemplated (p.12). At least 60 percent of each year's funds go to rural areas (p.4).

**Money and timing:** cost-reimbursable grants (p.3, p.8, p.22); period of performance 1 to 4 years (p.8); no limit on applications per applicant (p.7).

**Must have to apply (p.9-11):** SAM.gov registration before submitting (it takes several weeks) and a Unique Entity Identifier; submission through Grants.gov; the SF-424 family of forms; the **mandatory WCPP Application Template**; a project budget showing Federal and non-Federal amounts.

**How it is judged (p.12-17):** (1) reduction of wildlife-vehicle collisions: species, collision numbers and costs now and with the project, landscape context, the specific actions; (2) habitat connectivity: how many species and how much habitat; (3) secondary considerations: non-Federal contributions, economic and visitation benefits, innovative technology, education and outreach, a monitoring and research plan. Readiness is then rated on technical capacity, **financial completeness** (cost estimates from recent design, a **plan for cost overruns**, funding for **future operation and maintenance** of construction projects) and **permitting risk** (open litigation or agency opposition must be disclosed).

**Realistic preparation gaps:** State DOT has not yet agreed to administer the grant · State highway agency consultation not yet documented · no plan for cost overruns · no funding identified for long-term maintenance · no monitoring and research plan · SAM.gov registration not done.

## 2. FY 2027 Crash Causal Factors Program (FM-CCFP-27-001)

**Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration · posted · closes December 28, 2026, 5:00 PM EST (p.2) · $7 million in total, up to 30 awards, up to **$500,000** per award (p.7, p.18) · cooperative agreement (p.18)**

**Who can apply (p.3-4):** only entities in a **participating or alternate State** (30 participating, including Ohio, Texas, Virginia, Kentucky and Montana; 3 alternates), and **only the listed local agencies** (Indianapolis Metro PD, Louisville PD, Columbus PD, and specific police departments in Florida, Missouri, New York and Texas). All applicants face a review of their compatibility with the study. For-profit organizations and individuals are not eligible (p.4). Local agencies are strongly encouraged to apply directly or through their State's lead agency (p.5).

**What it funds (p.7-11):** collecting data for the **Heavy-Duty Truck Study**, which covers **fatal crashes involving Class 7 and 8 trucks** (over 26,000 pounds) (p.9). Three kinds of activity:
- **Crash response:** advanced reconstructions or post-crash investigations of those crashes. **Not** basic police crash reports, and **creating new reconstruction teams is not funded**: applicants must show existing capability, equipment, training and how long they have done this (p.9-10).
- **Post-crash inspection and data collection:** all funded investigations **must include inspections by North American Standard (NAS) Part A/B certified officers** (p.10). Personnel for those inspections and data work are fundable.
- **Equipment** directly tied to reconstruction and data gathering (for example brake-checking tools, event data recorder software and hardware, drones, total stations), **pro-rated** if it also serves non-truck crashes (p.10-11, p.20).
- **Overtime** is allowed, with a **historical basis for the estimate** (p.10). Routine vehicles and operating costs belong under the State's MCSAP formula grant (p.11).

**Rules:** no matching share (p.5); direct local applicants must certify six conditions from 49 CFR 350.405 and coordinate with the Lead State Agency so the proposal fits the State's CCFP plan (p.5); up to four projects per application and a 35-page narrative (p.12); period of performance is the year of award plus three years, with no reimbursement for crashes in the last 180 days (p.18).

**Must have to apply (p.5-6):** completed SF-424, SF-424A, SF-424B, lobbying certification (and SF-LLL if applicable), a complete budget narrative and project narrative, submitted by the deadline.

**Realistic preparation gaps:** too few NAS Part A/B certified officers (or not yet confirmed) · no historical basis for the overtime estimate · not yet coordinated with the Lead State Agency · budget narrative not written · equipment request not yet pro-rated.

## 3. State Process for Informing Consumers of Recalls Program (NHTSA-693JJ927R000007-FY27)

**National Highway Traffic Safety Administration · posted November 4, 2026 (p.2) · questions due December 1, 2026, 5 PM ET; applications due February 4, 2027, 5 PM ET (p.16) · up to $1.5 million in total, **3 to 5 awards of $300,000 to $500,000 each**, which NHTSA may change (p.1) · one application per eligible applicant (p.1)**

**Who can apply (p.5):** a **State agency with the authority to register motor vehicles** (the States and the District of Columbia, p.2) that has the infrastructure, staff and support to run the project. **A State that has already received an award under this program is ineligible.** Maryland received the earlier pilot grant (p.6); the notice does not say whether that counts, so a sample should avoid Maryland.

**What it funds (p.2, p.7):** a State process that (1) works out which vehicles have **open safety recalls** (for example by VIN), (2) **informs owners and lessees at the time of registration**, at no cost to them, and (3) **evaluates the results** by analyzing completed recalls. The State must include a method for analyzing recall completion data on an ongoing basis (p.7). Attitude surveys of owners are encouraged, not required (p.7).

**Timing (p.9):** about **33 months**: 6 months to set the system up, 24 months of continuous notification at registration, then 3 months to evaluate and report. A final report is due within 90 days of the end of the performance period (p.7-8). Quarterly reports on owners notified and recalls remedied (p.8).

**Rules:** no cost share, though other funding is encouraged (p.5); Buy America applies (p.9).

**What the application must contain (p.10-15):** a narrative of **10 pages or fewer** with applicant background (agency role and authority, infrastructure and capacity, relevant past performance, key personnel) and a project plan covering the **source of recall data and how it is integrated**, the **notification language** (the defect, the safety risk, and that dealers fix it free), **every registration touchpoint** (in person, online, mail, renewals, inspections), data elements kept per vehicle, **performance measurement beyond simple process measures**, a work breakdown and schedule, a **staffing plan**, quality control and risk mitigation, and travel. Also a budget in Excel with cost detail by year (p.13-14), SF-424, 424A, 424B, drug-free workplace and lobbying certifications (p.14-15), SAM.gov registration and a UEI (p.15).

**Realistic preparation gaps:** recall-data source or vendor not chosen yet · no method yet for analyzing completed recalls · no project manager or staffing plan · notification language not drafted · SAM.gov registration not current.

## 4. Innovative Traffic Safety Enforcement (ITSE) Grant Program (NHTSA-693JJ926R000024-FY26)

**National Highway Traffic Safety Administration · posted August 3, 2026 · up to $20 million in total; **8 to 15 awards of $500,000 to $2,000,000 each**, which NHTSA may change (p.1-2) · applications evaluated in **three phases**: submitted by 9/3/2026, by **3/1/2027** and by 9/1/2027, all due 5 PM EST; one application per eligible applicant per phase, and a revised application may be sent in a later phase (p.1)**

**Who can apply (p.6):** a State or local agency, authority, association, educational institution or nonprofit that shows the organizational capacity and experience to run the project. An applicant not selected in one phase may apply in a later one.

**What it funds (p.8-9):** **new, innovative countermeasures, or innovative enhancements to existing ones**, that reduce crashes from risky driving. The applicant **must name the focus area**: drug-impaired driving, alcohol-impaired driving, speeding, aggressive driving, occupant protection, distracted driving, or careless and negligent driving (especially in work zones, around commercial vehicles, in school zones and in congested areas) (p.8). Projects must be **evidence-based and data-driven**, and **scalable and transferable for use by State and local law enforcement** (p.9). Examples NHTSA gives: data-driven enforcement models using spatial and temporal analytics, training and education for law enforcement, community-based enforcement initiatives, and enforcement technologies. Any use of artificial intelligence must be described (p.9).

**What the application must show (p.8-10):** a **data-collection and analysis plan to evaluate effectiveness and allow replication**; goals written as SMART objectives; the intended audience; barriers and how to overcome them; performance measures (annual fatalities, serious injuries and crashes by road user type in the project area, costs, lessons learned).

**Rules:** no cost share (p.6); **period of performance up to 60 months** (p.11); Buy America applies and covered-technology sub-awards are prohibited (p.11); a 20-page narrative with a one-page cover page, SF-424, a Part 32 drug-free workplace statement and a Part 20 lobbying statement (p.12); SAM.gov registration before submitting (p.16). Cost data in Excel or Google Sheets comes from the Q&A document (August 20, 2026), not the notice.

**Realistic preparation gaps:** no evaluation partner or data-collection method yet · focus area or SMART objectives not defined · SAM.gov registration not done · no baseline crash data assembled · AI use not yet described.

## 5. FAA Aviation Research Grants Program (20-01)

**Federal Aviation Administration · posted · new applications accepted on a schedule: the next dates are **December 1, 2026** and **January 2, 2027** (p.20); optional white papers by November 1, 2026 and January 2, 2027 (p.19) · about $6 million a year in total, no minimum or maximum award (p.9) · grants and cooperative agreements (p.9)**

**Who can apply (p.11):** **colleges and universities**, and **other nonprofit research organizations** eligible under 501(c)(3) (such as research laboratories, consortia and professional or scientific societies). Individuals are not eligible. **State and local governments are not listed.** The FAA wants equitable geographic distribution, including Historically Black Colleges and Universities and other Minority Serving Institutions (p.11).

**What it funds (p.5-8):** **research** for the long-term growth of civil aviation and commercial space transportation, and on preventing catastrophic aircraft failures. Seven program areas (p.6): capacity and air traffic control technology; communications, navigation and surveillance; aviation weather; airports (pavement design, runway friction, snow and ice control, bird and wildlife control and similar research); **aircraft safety technology** (fire and cabin safety, structural safety and advanced materials, propulsion, aging aircraft, catastrophic failure prevention, safety risk analysis); human factors and aviation medicine; and systems science and operations research.

**What it will not fund:** **education, training or specific airport development** (p.14), and proposals **solely for buying equipment or constructing facilities** (p.18).

**Rules and money:** period of performance **12 to 60 months** (p.10); **cost sharing is not required**, but the FAA expects recipients to share costs and an offer "may be a significant factor" (p.12); continuation applications cannot compete with new ones (p.9, p.11); applicants are strongly encouraged to contact the relevant FAA program manager before applying (p.9).

**Must have to apply (p.12, p.14-18, p.22):** SAM.gov registration, a Unique Entity Identifier and Grants.gov; the **SF 424 Research and Related** form; a research budget with narrative; senior and key person profiles with biographical sketches; disclosure of all current and pending support; a 200 to 300 word project summary; a description of work; a copy of the **indirect cost agreement**; the **Non-Discrimination Assurances** form. A white paper (3 pages at most) is optional (p.14). Judged equally on **intrinsic value, relevance to the FAA mission, technical soundness and research performance competence**, and failing any one may make a proposal ineligible (p.22).

**Realistic preparation gaps:** SAM.gov registration not done · indirect cost agreement not in place · current and pending support not yet compiled · FAA program manager not yet contacted · key person profiles not written.

## What this changes about the samples already in the app

The samples written earlier today matched each program's eligible applicant, but several details are wrong against the notices:

- **Recalls:** the sample requests **$1.2 million**, but the notice anticipates **$300,000 to $500,000** per award. It also never mentions naming a recall-data source, the notification language or a project manager, which the application is built around.
- **ITSE:** the sample requests **$2.4 million**, above the anticipated **$500,000 to $2,000,000**; it does not name a **focus area** (the notice requires one: it describes two, speeding and impaired driving) or the **phase** it would apply in.
- **Crash Causal Factors:** consistent with the notice (existing unit, $450,000 under the $500,000 ceiling, no match), but it does not use the overtime or Lead State Agency points.
- **Wildlife Crossings:** uses the State DOT consultation and the 20 percent match, but not the rule that the **State DOT must administer the grant** for a local applicant.
- **FAA:** a fair fit; it does not use the indirect cost agreement or the ban on equipment-only proposals as written.
