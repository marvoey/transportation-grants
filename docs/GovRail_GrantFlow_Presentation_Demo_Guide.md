# GovRail + GrantFlow AI

*Presentation & Demo Playbook | Internal working guide, not a submission artifact*

Purpose: Deliver a credible 2–5 minute Big Build demonstration that makes the GovRail innovation tangible, proves GrantFlow works today, and leads with productivity at scale. The competition deck requires a talking-head component, a demo walkthrough, and impact; a working demonstration should take priority over slides.

## 1. The story judges should remember

**Problem:** Government staff spend hours translating complex funding notices into project-specific answers; hundreds of applicants face the same deadline-driven process.

**Innovation:** GovRail combines LLM reasoning with deterministic checks, evidence grounding, and guided user interaction. It guides work instead of producing an unqualified AI answer.

**Proof:** GrantFlow uses GovRail’s approach in two working flows: Find Grants and Assess a Project. Spend the majority of demo time on assessment.

**Impact:** Modeled reduction from 6–10 hours to 2–3 hours per preliminary assessment; roughly 800 applicant hours potentially freed at 50% adoption in a 300-application cycle. Federal benefits are potential downstream effects.

## 3. Demo preparation: build a reliable path

- Choose a public, currently accessible DOT opportunity that GrantFlow has successfully prepared. Confirm the official documents and Q&A attachments were processed; do not imply coverage of documents the pipeline did not ingest.

- Use a fictional but credible transportation project. State it is fictional; avoid CUI, real applicant records, and restricted data.

- Rehearse a discovery-to-assessment transition. If document preparation takes time or can fail, pre-prepare the opportunity and explain that step honestly.

- Select one source-backed assessment finding that is clear on screen, one unresolved question, and one requirement that needs specialist review. Verify citations manually.

- Keep a backup screen recording and screenshots of actual prototype outputs. Avoid representing mocked or edited outputs as live results.

- Hide development consoles, browser notifications, secret keys, and personal data. Enlarge browser text for screen capture.

## 4. The key assessment moment

A suitable sample: a city wants to purchase traffic analytics software and run a speeding-enforcement pilot. A NHTSA program may support innovative enforcement but not standalone equipment procurement. In the demo, show how the assistant flags this distinction, cites the official clarification, and asks whether the project is primarily a procurement or an evaluated enforcement demonstration. Use the specific grant only after confirming that the currently prepared source package includes the clarification.

This is the “GovRail moment”: the LLM understands ambiguous project language, deterministic checks constrain explicit conditions, the finding points to evidence, and the user decides what to investigate next. Do not demonstrate an automatic eligibility verdict.

## 5. Productivity model: what to display

**Per assessment:** Manual 6–10 hours; AI-assisted target 2–3 hours; approximately 70% reduction at the midpoint; 5–6 hours potentially freed.

**One funding round:** 300 applications, 50% adoption = 150 assisted assessments; 150 × 5.5 modeled hours ≈ 825, rounded to about 800 applicant staff hours.

**Federal side:** If assisted applicants would otherwise generate 40 exchanges per 100 and those exchanges fall by 25%, ≈15 exchanges might be avoided. At 1 hour each, ≈15 federal staff hours. This is an illustrative hypothesis, not a measured rate.

**Scale evidence:** Prior research: SMART received 308 applications in FY 2024; RAISE received 1,048 eligible applications in FY 2024. Verify the original DOT/GAO sources before placing source citations on public-facing slides.

**Timing:** Show a fixed NOFO deadline and the subsequent concentrated federal review phase. Do not claim that submissions actually arrive predominantly in the last week without timestamp evidence.

## 6. Claims and evidence discipline

- Say “modeled,” “scenario-based,” “potential,” and “subject to validation” for the time and savings estimates. Do not say “we saved 800 hours.”

- Explain that federal-side benefits are plausible downstream effects of better-informed applicants, not a currently demonstrated federal reviewer function.

- Distinguish staff capacity freed from realized cash budget savings. Exclude AI processing costs from gross labor models unless explicitly calculated.

- GovRail is demonstrated as a hybrid AI guidance approach in two GrantFlow workflows; do not claim it is already an independent platform or deployable SDK.

- Do not claim GrantFlow performs final eligibility certification, award forecasting, automatic submissions, or actual federal application review.

- Capture a real screen recording of the working prototype. Label scenario project details and all savings projections clearly.

## 7. Evidence-gathering test (if time permits)

- Lock the project profile and NOFO source package so manual and AI-assisted participants review the same evidence.

- Time the manual tasks: document review, rule extraction, project-specific interpretation, questions, and written findings.

- Time GrantFlow-assisted tasks, including user verification and document preparation where attributable.

- Have an independent reviewer score missed requirements, false conflicts, citation correctness, and the usefulness of follow-up questions.

- Present observed results separately from modeled assumptions; if no test is completed, retain the estimate label.
