Three ways to work

Find grants

Describe your project in your own words and answer a few short questions. You get a short list of U.S. Department of Transportation opportunities from Simpler.Grants.gov, with the reasons each one may fit and what to watch out for.

Assess a project

Choose a funding notice that has been prepared, describe your project, and get a preliminary read: what the notice supports, where it may conflict, what you still need to prepare, and what is unknown. Each finding points to its source document.

How GrantFlow stays reliable

GrantFlow is built on GovRail, an approach that pairs a language model with plain, checkable code. The model interprets and guides; code enforces the rules; people make the decisions.

  • Model reasoning Reads your project description and the notice's complex language, asks clarifying questions and explains what it found.
  • Deterministic checks Apply explicit rules in code, so numbers and conditions are checked rather than guessed.
  • Evidence grounding Links each finding to the official document and keeps missing information separate from real conflicts.
  • Guided questions Use the gaps it finds to decide what to ask or investigate next.
Preliminary guidance only. GrantFlow does not make eligibility determinations or funding decisions. Confirm anything that matters with the program office.
